Demand Growth

Data centers, electrification, and industrial demand are pushing U.S. electricity consumption up again after roughly two decades of essentially flat growth.

  • AI-driven data center demand. AI training and inference workloads are the most visible driver of the recent demand growth reversal, with individual data center campuses now requesting hundreds of megawatts to multiple gigawatts of power, comparable to a mid-size city. This has caught utilities and grid planners off guard after two decades of planning around flat demand growth.
  • Hyperscaler power deals. Hyperscalers are increasingly signing power purchase agreements directly with generators, including nuclear plant restarts and new gas and renewable projects, partly to lock in supply and partly to skip the years-long interconnection queues that a conventional grid connection would require. Some are even considering building and operating their own generation behind the meter, bypassing the grid connection question almost entirely.
  • EV adoption. Electric vehicle adoption adds a second, slower-moving but larger source of structural demand growth, since transportation electrification touches a much larger share of total energy use than data centers do, even though its growth is more gradual and more spread out geographically.
  • Industrial electrification. Efforts to electrify industrial processes and heating (replacing natural gas furnaces and industrial heat sources with electric alternatives) are a further source of demand growth, driven both by decarbonization goals and, in some cases, by the relative cost of electricity versus gas in specific end uses.
  • Forecast reversals. Utility long-term demand forecasts, many of which assumed flat or even declining electricity demand as efficiency gains offset economic growth, have been revised sharply upward across much of the U.S. in the past few years. This forecast reversal is itself part of why grid infrastructure, generation permitting, and reliability have all become more urgent policy topics simultaneously.
