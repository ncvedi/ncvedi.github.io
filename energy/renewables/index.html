Renewables

Solar and wind cost curves, intermittency, and the storage buildout needed to make renewable generation dispatchable rather than just cheap.

  • Solar's shifting constraint. Solar module costs have fallen so far that in many markets the constraint on new solar is no longer generation cost but interconnection, land availability, and permitting timelines. This shift means policy and process improvements now matter more for solar deployment speed than further cost declines in the panels themselves.
  • Storage pairing. Battery storage costs are following a similar downward cost curve to solar and are increasingly being paired directly with solar projects to shift output into the evening peak hours when solar generation has already dropped off but demand remains high. This pairing is what allows solar-plus-storage to start competing on dispatchability, not just on levelized cost of pure generation.
  • Wind's harder few years. Wind, particularly offshore wind, has had a rougher stretch than solar, with several high-profile projects facing cost overruns, cancellations, and financing troubles as interest rates rose and supply chains for turbines and specialized vessels stayed tight. Offshore wind's economics are especially sensitive to financing costs given how capital-intensive and long-dated the projects are.
  • Policy support and its uncertainty. Tax credits and other subsidies (like those in the Inflation Reduction Act) have been a major driver of renewable project economics in the U.S., and changes to that policy support create significant uncertainty for project pipelines that are planned years in advance. This policy sensitivity is a bigger swing factor for renewable investment than most commodity price movements.
  • Curtailment. In regions where renewable buildout has outpaced transmission and storage capacity, grid operators increasingly have to curtail (turn down) solar and wind output during periods of oversupply, effectively wasting generation capacity that was already paid for. Curtailment rates are a useful, if underappreciated, signal of where transmission and storage investment is lagging behind generation buildout.
