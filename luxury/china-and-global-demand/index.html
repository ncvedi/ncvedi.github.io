China and Global Demand

Why the Chinese consumer became the single biggest swing factor in the luxury industry's growth, and what its recent slowdown has exposed about how concentrated that growth had become.

  • The Chinese consumer's outsized weight. Chinese buyers, counting both mainland spending and outbound travel purchases, came to represent roughly a third or more of global luxury spending by the late 2010s. Growth in this single cohort was the largest driver of the industry's decade-long expansion before the pandemic.
  • The post-COVID slowdown. Brands broadly expected a sharp rebound in Chinese demand once travel and consumption normalized after the pandemic. Instead, property market distress, slower income growth, and weaker consumer confidence produced a much deeper and more prolonged pullback than most forecasts assumed, catching brands' inventory and store expansion plans off guard.
  • Duty-free and travel retail. Hainan Island's duty-free zone and outbound shopping in destinations like Japan and Korea are both major sales channels for Chinese consumers, and currency swings — particularly a weak yen making Japan more attractive — have shifted where purchases actually happen even when underlying demand from Chinese buyers is roughly stable.
  • Diversification away from China dependence. Brands have leaned harder into the U.S. and other Asian markets since the slowdown, with Japan benefiting further from tourist spending drawn by the weak yen and India increasingly discussed as a long-term growth market, partly as a deliberate hedge after the China slowdown exposed how concentrated the industry's growth engine had become.
  • Aspirational vs. top-of-pyramid demand. The segment hit hardest by the Chinese slowdown has been aspirational, entry-level luxury buyers pulling back on discretionary spending, while demand from ultra-high-net-worth buyers for the very top tier — haute couture, rare watches, fine jewelry — has stayed comparatively resilient, a bifurcation increasingly visible across the sector's quarterly earnings.
