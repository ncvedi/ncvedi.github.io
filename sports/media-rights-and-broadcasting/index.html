Media Rights and Broadcasting

Why live sports has become the most valuable content category in television even as the rest of the TV business erodes, and how streaming is reshaping who bids for it.

  • Live sports as the last appointment viewing. As streaming fragmented scripted entertainment and eroded linear TV ratings broadly, live sports remained one of the few things audiences still reliably watch in real time. That's made sports rights more valuable to broadcasters precisely as the rest of their programming business erodes around it.
  • Escalating rights fees. The NFL's current media rights deals, spanning CBS, Fox, NBC, ESPN, and Amazon, are worth over 100 billion dollars combined across roughly a decade. Each renewal cycle has come in well above the last despite audience fragmentation elsewhere, because rights holders are bidding for one of the only guaranteed live audiences left on television.
  • Streaming entrants reshaping the bidding. Amazon (Thursday Night Football), Apple (MLS Season Pass, some MLB), and Netflix (NFL Christmas games, boxing) have all entered sports rights bidding as a subscriber acquisition and retention tool, willing to bid up prices in ways traditional broadcasters, constrained by ad-revenue economics, sometimes can't match.
  • Fragmentation and fan frustration. As rights get split across more platforms, a fan following a single team across a season might now need cable, ESPN+, Peacock, Apple TV+, and Amazon Prime just to watch every game. That friction has grown alongside cord-cutting, a tension leagues are aware of but have mostly prioritized maximizing total rights revenue over.
  • Regional sports networks in distress. The traditional regional sports network model, exemplified by Diamond Sports Group's Bally Sports networks, which funded a large share of local MLB and NHL team revenue, is collapsing as cord-cutting erodes the cable bundle underneath it; Diamond filed for bankruptcy in 2023. That's forcing leagues to experiment with direct-to-consumer streaming for local games, an unresolved structural problem more urgent than the marquee national deals get credit for.
