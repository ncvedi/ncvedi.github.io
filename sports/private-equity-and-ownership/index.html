Private Equity and Institutional Ownership

Why leagues that spent decades keeping institutional money out of team ownership have opened the door to it, and what still makes sports an unusual asset class for a private equity fund.

  • Historical bans on institutional capital. Major U.S. leagues traditionally required owners to be individuals or small ownership groups, largely to keep control concentrated, accountable, and, in theory, genuinely invested in the team rather than treating it as a purely financial holding.
  • The opening to private equity. As valuations climbed into the billions, leagues relaxed these rules one by one: the NBA approved institutional investors in 2020, with MLB and the NHL following, allowing funds like Arctos, Ares, and Dyal (now part of Blue Owl) to take passive minority stakes. This gave existing owners liquidity options and widened the pool of capital able to support ever-higher franchise prices.
  • Passive stakes, not control. League rules generally still cap private equity ownership at a minority, non-controlling stake and restrict funds from owning stakes in competing teams within the same league, an attempt to preserve the traditional governance model while still accessing institutional capital.
  • Soccer's more permissive model. European soccer has gone further, with private equity and sovereign wealth capital, including Gulf-linked investment and CVC's stakes in the commercial rights of several leagues, taking larger and sometimes controlling positions. That's part of why soccer club ownership has become a more geopolitically charged topic than U.S. sports ownership generally is.
  • Fund economics and exit questions. Private equity funds investing in sports teams face an unusual problem for the asset class: there's no established path to a full exit or IPO the way there typically is in private equity, since any sale requires league approval and the pool of eventual buyers is still mostly other wealthy individuals or funds. That makes both the exit timeline and mechanism far less certain than in most private equity strategies.
