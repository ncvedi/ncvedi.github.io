Global Expansion

How U.S. leagues are chasing growth outside a saturated domestic market, and why European soccer's inverse push into the U.S. tells a similar story from the other direction.

  • U.S. leagues playing abroad. The NFL now plays a growing slate of regular-season games each year in London, Munich and Frankfurt, and other international cities, explicitly to build fan bases and media rights value outside the saturated domestic market. The NBA and MLB have run similar, though smaller, international game programs.
  • International media rights as a growth lever. With domestic media rights deals already at or near what the market can bear, leagues increasingly see international audiences, particularly in growing media markets, as the more available source of long-term rights revenue growth going forward.
  • European soccer's inverse expansion into the U.S. Premier League and La Liga clubs, among others, have built large U.S. fan bases partly through preseason tours and greater U.S. broadcast availability. The U.S. is now one of the most valuable single markets for a league like the Premier League despite having no domestic team to anchor that interest.
  • The 2026 World Cup as an inflection point. The U.S., Mexico, and Canada-hosted 2026 World Cup is widely viewed by leagues and investors as a potential catalyst for U.S. soccer interest and MLS's next valuation and media rights cycle, similar to the lasting bumps in domestic engagement that past host countries have seen.
  • Expansion franchises and market selection. When leagues do expand, as MLS has done steadily and the NFL last did in 2002, expansion fees have risen enormously with each new round, both reflecting the scarcity value discussed elsewhere and because leagues use expansion fee revenue as a way to enrich existing owners without touching shared media revenue.
